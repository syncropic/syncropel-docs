Guardians are people you trust to help you get back into your workspace if you lose every device. No single
guardian can get in alone: you choose how many of them must agree. When a recovery starts, it waits before it
finishes, and you can stop it from any of your devices.

You set guardians up on the command line. The browser doesn't offer guardians.

## 1. Choose how many must agree

```bash
spl account rung set --guardians 2 --of 3
```

This says three people will hold a part, and any two of them together can help you back in. A recovery then
waits 7 days before it finishes (`--wait` sets a different wait, in seconds). Check what's in force with
`spl account rung show`.

## 2. Invite each guardian

Make an invite for each person, with a name you'll recognize:

```bash
spl session request POST /v1/account/guardians/invite --data '{"label":"Ana"}'
```

The answer gives you a link for that person. Send it to them yourself, in a way you trust.

## 3. Your guardian accepts, on their own computer

Your guardian runs:

```bash
spl guardian accept --home <your workspace's address> --token <the token from their link> --name Ana
```

It shows them their guardian check:

> Your guardian check is {check}. Read it to the person who invited you when they ask for it.

## 4. Ask them to read you their check, then add them

Ask your guardian to read you the check on their screen, by phone or in person. Don't take it from the same
message the invite went in. Then type it in:

```bash
spl account guardians arm --slot <slot> --check "<what they read you>"
```

They're added only if what you typed matches the guardian who accepted. If it doesn't, you'll see:

> Ana wasn't added. Someone else may have opened the invite link. Make a new invite and give it to Ana yourself.

If you leave out `--check`, the command asks you to:

> Ask your guardian to read you their check, then add it with --check.

An accepted guardian who isn't added within a day lapses, and you make a new invite.

## 5. Your guardian keeps their part

Your guardian picks up their part and keeps a copy somewhere safe:

```bash
spl guardian pickup --name Ana --out ana-part.json
```

One part alone reveals nothing. From time to time your guardian can check they still hold it with
`spl guardian check --name Ana`.

## If you lose every device

Start a recovery from your new device with `spl face recover --request`, and ask your guardians to approve it.
When enough have approved, the wait begins. If it was stopped from one of your devices and you didn't mean to
stop it, start a new recovery with `spl face recover --request --new`. Your guardians will need to approve
again, and the wait starts over.
