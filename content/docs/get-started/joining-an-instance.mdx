title: Joining a workspace
description: Someone sent you an invite link. What the page asks, what name to pick, who holds your signing key, and where your token goes.
---

You've been sent a link that looks like `https://<workspace-address>/i/inv_...`. Someone who runs a Syncropel workspace is inviting you to join it as a **member**. That is more than an access token: it gives you your own identity on their workspace. Your reviews are signed with it, trust builds up on it, and you can leave at any time.

This page is for you, the person invited. The inviter's side is the [Add a teammate tutorial](/docs/tutorials/add-a-teammate).

## What the link shows

The invite page displays, before you commit to anything:

- **Who sent the invite**: the workspace and the person.
- **What you'd be granted**: what you can do (usually read and add records) and how long the membership lasts.
- **When the link expires**: invite links last from a few hours to a day; if it has expired, ask for a new one.

Nothing happens until you confirm. If the invite has been revoked or already used up, the page says so straight away.

## Choosing your member name

The page asks you to pick a name: lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, starting with a letter or digit, up to 63 characters. `mora`, `dan-k`, `ops2` are all fine.

The name becomes your identity on this workspace (`did:sync:user:<name>`). It appears in the member list, on your reviews, with your trust, and when you leave. Pick something you're happy to see in an audit log for years.

If the name is already taken by an existing member, the invite is refused, so an invite link can never take over someone else's identity. (One exception: an admin can send an invite *for your name* so you can renew your own membership. If you were told "this link renews your access", that's what is happening.)

## Who holds your signing key

The workspace creates and holds your signing key, and signs on your behalf. In plain terms:

- **The person who runs the workspace holds your signing key.** Your identity here is only as trustworthy as they are. You cannot hold the key yourself: asking to do so is refused.
- **Membership works both ways.** Both you and the workspace sign off when it starts, and either side can end it: they can remove you, and **you can end your own membership at any time**. You are not locked in.
- **Everything is on the record.** Your joining, what you were allowed to do, your reviews, and any ending of your membership are all saved as records you can inspect.

If that tradeoff doesn't work for you, don't accept the invite: joining someone's workspace means trusting whoever runs it.

## Where your token goes

After you confirm, the page shows you a token **once**. Your tools use it to sign in.

For the CLI:

```bash
spl token save <token>
```

That writes `~/.syncro/token` (readable only by you), and every `spl` command for the workspace sends it automatically. For an SDK or HTTP, send it in the `Authorization: Bearer` header. See [Service accounts and tokens](/docs/get-started/service-accounts) for the order `spl` looks for a token (`--token` flag, `SPL_TOKEN` environment variable, saved file).

## How your token relates to your membership

Your token is tied to your membership. That has two effects:

1. **The token expires sooner than your membership** (30 days by default) and can be renewed without touching your membership. Your membership lasts longer (180 days by default).
2. **When your membership ends (it expires, you end it, or they do), every token under it stops working.** Your next request gets `401 GRANT_EXPIRED` or `401 GRANT_REVOKED`. A token never outlives the membership.

A new device is not the end of your access either: open any thread, press **keep this access**, and the key it shows re-opens the workspace as you from any browser (see [Keeping your access](/docs/guides/keeping-your-access)). An expired membership is not the end of your identity: an admin sends a renewal invite for your name, you accept it, and you are the same member (same identity, same history) with a new membership and token.

## Your row on the roster

You can see your own membership at any time:

```bash
spl member list
```

You'll see exactly one row: your own, with status, scopes, and expiry. That's not an error: you only see your own row, and only admins see other members. Likewise, you only see the permissions that involve you.
