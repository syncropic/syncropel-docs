Open a conversation in your workspace and type, and your assistant answers. On a hosted workspace it's there from your very first message. It's called the **chief of staff** ("the chief").

## What the chief does

- **Answers from what's in your workspace.** It reads what you're allowed to read: your conversations, tasks, files and decisions. It answers from those, not from guesses.
- **Asks you when it needs to.** If something needs your decision, it raises a clear question instead of deciding for you.
- **Does jobs, not just answers.** Ask it to "summarize last week's decisions" or "tidy up the tasks tagged beta", and it works through the steps in front of you.

## How a conversation works

```
 you type ──▶ your message is saved in the conversation
                     │
                     ▼
          the chief reads the conversation so far
                     │
                     ▼
     its reply is saved in the same conversation ──▶ you see it
```

Everything stays in the conversation, so it's still there when you come back tomorrow, and the chief picks up where you left off. There's nothing to start or save.

## When a question turns into a job

Some messages need work, not just a reply. When that happens:

- **The work is done as you.** It uses your permissions, so it can never do more than you could yourself, and what it changes is recorded as done on your behalf.
- **Each step shows up in the conversation**, so you can watch it happen.
- **It doesn't mark its own homework.** The workspace checks the result against what you asked for before calling it done.

## If the chief doesn't answer

- **On Free, the starting allowance may be used up.** Move the workspace to Pro on [account.syncropel.com](https://account.syncropel.com) to keep going.
- **On Pro, this month's share may be used up.** The assistant comes back when more is available.
- **Your workspace may be waking up** after a quiet spell. Wait a few seconds and try again.

## For developers

The chief is an ordinary assistant, defined by a [blueprint](/docs/concepts/blueprints) like any other, so you can read it, change it or replace it. A turn that needs tools runs through a governed [work loop](/docs/concepts/work-loop); to start one yourself and sit with it, use [`spl run`](/docs/guides/running-a-goal). Turn-by-turn escalation is on by default, and you can turn it off for a given assistant (see Under the hood below).

<Callout type="info">
Workspaces before v0.216 had a separate built-in assistant called Scribe (and a sibling orientation agent, the Guide), configured with `spl config scribe`. Those are retired; old records stay readable as history.
</Callout>

### Turning the assistant on when you self-host

A self-hosted workspace starts deliberately blank: it reacts to nothing until you
choose what runs on it. Two ways to give it a voice.

```bash
# At init: seed a ready-to-use setup that installs the chief.
spl init --preset recommended    # presets: recommended | blank

# Any time later, on an already-initialized instance:
spl blueprint install <path-to-blueprint> --accept-plan <hash>
```

Every preset except `blank` installs the chief. If you initialized bare and the
assistant seems silent, this is why, and nothing is broken: install the chief
through the blueprint door with the command above.

A workspace on your own machine has no model until you give it a key (`spl config set-key anthropic <your key>`, then restart the daemon); until then the chief is installed but silent, and `spl run` says so rather than failing quietly.

### The assistant is silent on a workspace you run yourself

If you send a message and nothing comes back, work through this in order:

1. **You initialized bare and never installed the chief.** The most common
   cause on a self-host. Run `spl blueprint install <path-to-blueprint> --accept-plan <hash>`, or
   re-init with `--preset recommended`.
2. **No inference backend, or the backend is rejecting calls.** An expired key
   or an exhausted quota means the reply call fails. Run `spl doctor`: it
   surfaces inference-configuration problems.
3. **The message landed on a non-conversational thread.** The chief answers on
   conversational threads. A record emitted onto a task thread is expected to
   stay quiet.
4. **Still stuck?** See [Troubleshooting](/docs/get-started/troubleshooting), or
   inspect the thread directly with `spl thread records <thread-id>` to confirm
   your message record was written.

### Under the hood

For readers working with the records directly:

- The chief is an actor registered on the workspace. Your message is a
  `core.user.message.v1` record, and the chief builds its context by reading
  the thread's records.
- Records an escalated turn writes are signed by your actor DID, not the
  chief's.
- An escalated turn's progress is written as `core.work.turn.v1`,
  `core.work.tool_call.v1` and `core.work.loop_outcome.v1` records.
- To turn escalation off for one actor, set `conversation_escalation: false` on
  its `core.engine.actor_adapter.v1` record.
