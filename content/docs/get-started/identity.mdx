Your Syncropel identity **follows you everywhere**. The same identity owns your content whether you reach it from your laptop, your phone, the web, or a workspace you run yourself. Nothing you create is tied to a single device. It's tied to *you*.

This page explains how your identity stays the same across devices, and how to back it up so you never lose access.

## One identity, many devices

When you sign in (on the web at syncropel.com or by connecting `spl` on a machine you maintain), you get the same identity every time. Signing in on a brand-new device gives you back the *same* identity, with the *same* content, automatically. There's no per-device setup and nothing to copy between machines for everyday use.

**Lost a device? Just sign in again.** Your content is owned by your sign-in identity, not by any machine. A stolen laptop or a reinstalled phone costs you nothing: sign in on the next device and everything you own is there. You don't need a recovery phrase for this. Day to day, all you need is to be able to sign in.

That is the story for a workspace **you own**. For a workspace somebody else let you into (a project, a teammate's workspace), your way back on a new device is a key you keep yourself: see [Keeping your access](/docs/guides/keeping-your-access). The recovery phrase on this page protects the identity of whoever runs a workspace; a kept key re-opens one workspace for one person. They are different things, and you may need both.

You can see your current identity any time:

```bash
spl identity show
```

It prints your identity, the method behind it, and the device you're signing from.

## Linked sign-in methods

You can have more than one way to sign in (for example an email address and a Google account), all pointing at the same identity. Adding a second sign-in method never creates a second identity; it's just another door into the one you already have. Manage these from your account settings on the web.

## Your recovery phrase

Everyday access depends on signing in. But your identity also has a **recovery phrase**: 24 ordinary words that are your identity at its root. With it, you can rebuild your identity on any device, without any sign-in provider.

<Callout type="info">
The recovery phrase is available on workspaces that use **master identity mode**. On a machine you look after yourself, turn it on with `spl init --master` (or set `SPL_IDENTITY_MODE=master` before `spl init`). On a workspace without it, the commands below tell you they aren't available. Your everyday sign-in continuity (above) is unaffected.
</Callout>

Reveal your recovery phrase like this:

```bash
spl identity backup --i-understand
```

<Callout type="warn">
Your recovery phrase is a secret. Anyone who has it can restore your identity. The command prints it **only to your own terminal** and never sends it anywhere. Write it down and store it offline: on paper, in a safe, or in a password manager. Do not paste it into chat, email, or a screenshot.
</Callout>

If you'd rather not keep the whole phrase in one place, you can split it into **five shares, any three of which** can rebuild it, useful for storing pieces in separate locations or with people you trust:

```bash
spl identity backup --i-understand --shamir
```

Keep at least three of the five shares safe. Fewer than three reveal nothing.

## Recovering on a new device

If you set up a fresh device, lose a machine, or simply want your identity on another computer, restore it from your recovery phrase:

```bash
spl identity recover --phrase "word1 word2 ... word24"
```

Run this on a fresh install. Your identity comes back **exactly as it was**: same identity, same ownership of your content. If you split your phrase into shares instead, supply any three:

```bash
spl identity recover --shamir-share "<share-1>" --shamir-share "<share-2>" --shamir-share "<share-3>"
```

The same phrase always rebuilds the same identity, on any device.

## Why this matters

- **Sign-in is convenience; the recovery phrase is ownership.** Day to day, you just sign in and your stuff is there. But if you ever lose a sign-in method, the recovery phrase lets you get your identity, and everything you own, back.
- **No one can lock you out.** Because only you hold the phrase, your access doesn't depend on any company or sign-in provider.
- **Back it up once, early.** The best time to save your recovery phrase is right after you first set up your identity. Do it before you create anything you'd hate to lose.

## What to do right now

1. If your workspace runs master identity mode, run `spl identity backup --i-understand` and write the 24 words down offline.
2. Store them somewhere you'll still have in a year, not just on the device you're using now.
3. Optionally split them into shares (`--shamir`) and keep the pieces in different places.

With your recovery phrase saved, you can always get your identity back, on any device.
