## Overview

<Callout type="info">
Automatic ranking by trust is a known limit: you choose which Faculty gets work with [routing rules](/docs/guides/routing-rules), and you can read each Faculty's [trust](/docs/concepts/trust). This page describes how the ranking works.
</Callout>

When more than one [Faculty](/docs/concepts/faculty) can handle the same kind of work, something has to decide which one gets it. The calibrated marketplace decides on **earned trust**: work goes to the candidate with the highest [trust](/docs/concepts/trust) for that area of work, measured only on outcomes that independent evaluators judged.

It is not a popularity contest, a price auction or a preference list. The only currency is verified results. A Faculty whose work is accepted rises, and one whose work is rejected falls.

## It ignores what a Faculty is made of

The marketplace ranks candidates by their trust in the relevant area and nothing else. A solver, a language model and a script are ranked the same way.

So a non-language-model Faculty competes on equal terms. If a cheap exact solver produces better-judged results than a language model on a class of problems, that class of work goes to the solver because the evidence put it on top.

For example, an exact solver and a weak heuristic both declare they handle the same problem. Independent verdicts grade their results and their trust moves apart. A new problem of that kind, naming no specific Faculty, goes to the one with the most trust.

## How a winner is chosen

1. **Gather candidates.** Every Faculty that declares it can handle this kind of work is in the running.
2. **Rank by trust.** Each candidate is scored by its earned trust in that area (see [Trust](/docs/concepts/trust)).
3. **Give the work to the best.** The highest-trust candidate gets it.

Two rules keep this fair:

- **New candidates start at zero.** A Faculty with no verdicts cannot leapfrog a proven one just by being deployed. It has to earn its standing first.
- **Ties favor the current one.** When scores tie, the Faculty already doing the work keeps it.

## Why it pays off

As evidence builds up:

- Work moves to whatever is genuinely best at it, often something cheaper and more predictable than a language model.
- Proven Faculties get more work, which produces more evidence.
- You can add new Faculties safely. They only take over where they actually win.

A Faculty that was good but starts producing worse-judged results drifts down as its recent [trust](/docs/concepts/trust) falls, and work moves to a better candidate.
