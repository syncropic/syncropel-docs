## Overview

Everything in Syncropel is a [record](/docs/concepts/records). The Studio, the web app you open for your workspace, builds what you see from your records every time. It does not keep a separate copy of your screens.

So the whole Studio is only **three shapes**. Learn these and you have learned the Studio:

- A **sentence** is an act with holes. It is the only way anything is written.
- A **ledger** is a list of rows with actions. It is how you read what is there.
- An **address** is where you are, and you can type it. It is how you move, cite, and share.

A sentence is like a command line that shows you its blanks. A ledger is like a spreadsheet that knows what its rows can do. An address is like a URL that means something. There is no fourth kind of screen to learn.

## The sentence: how you write

You never fill in a form in the Studio. You complete a sentence. Type `/` and the surface offers the acts you can perform here. Pick one, and it lifts a sentence with holes for what it still needs:

```text
invite  @who?  as  a guest  for  how long?  to  [[ the night ]]
```

Each hole knows what can fill it. The `@who?` hole offers people, the `for` hole understands "a week" or "3 days", and the `[[ ]]` hole offers threads. You fill the holes, and the surface shows you, in plain words, exactly what the act will do before you commit it. Nothing is written until you send.

This is the **only way to write**. There are no hidden buttons or forms behind menus. If something can be done, it is a sentence.

The lexicon is small. Five marks start a hole or an act:

| Type | To |
|---|---|
| `/` | start an act, the sentences you can complete here |
| `[[` | name a thread |
| `@` | name a person |
| `#` | reach a place or a saved view |
| `?` | ask, a text hole, the words themselves |

## The ledger: how you read

A ledger is a table built from your records. It has columns, rows grouped into sections, a one-line summary, and **actions on its rows**. A row in an invites ledger can be revoked. A row in a files ledger can be opened or replaced. It shows you what you can do to each row, and offers an action only when it can actually be done.

Two rules make a ledger trustworthy:

- **Every count tells you whether it is complete.** A count that might be short shows a `+` (`5+ coming`) instead of pretending to be final. When the full set is known, the `+` disappears.
- **A proposal never renders as settled.** A value someone suggested but you have not agreed to looks different from a fact.

The places down the left of the Studio (your call, everything, files, the people) are ledgers too, covering the whole workspace instead of one thread.

## The address: where you are

The bar at the bottom of the Studio is not a breadcrumb. It is the **address**, and its value is real. You can read it, type it, and hand it to someone.

```text
place  ›  thread  ›  view   @moment   as guest
```

- The **place** is where you are standing.
- The **thread** is what you have open.
- The **view** is which view of it is open. `› invites` says you are reading the invites ledger of this thread.
- **`@moment`** appears only when you are looking at a past moment rather than now.
- **`as guest`** appears when you are previewing the surface as someone else would see it. See [previewing as a guest](#previewing-as-a-guest).

Because the address is a real value, you can use it to **cite** and **share**. Copy the address, send it, and the person who opens it lands exactly where you were. A public link is a different kind of address on purpose, so what you hand to a stranger is not the address of the room you are in.

## The loop, and that it closes

The three shapes are not three separate features. They are one loop:

1. You **read** a ledger.
2. A row's verb lifts a **sentence** with the row already bound.
3. You complete and send it, and a record is written.
4. The ledger **updates** with the new record, and you are reading again.

Every action in the Studio is one lap of that loop, and the address is where you are while you do it. The same fact never appears in two places under two names.

## Previewing as a guest

Before you share a thread, you can look at it the way the person you share with will. Open a thread you can share and choose **see it as a guest**. The page narrows to exactly what a guest link discloses: only the records that link admits, rendered through the guest's own read-only view. A banner says you are looking, not editing. Nothing there is yours to change while you preview, and **stop** returns you to your own view.

It is not a mock-up. The preview shows what a guest will actually get, both the records and the screen around them. Look before you share.
