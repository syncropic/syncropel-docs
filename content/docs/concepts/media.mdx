[Your library](/docs/concepts/your-library) is about *keeping* things. This page is about *playing* them: how a track sounds or a video plays. Syncropel treats audio and video as one thing: **content that is presented to you and that you interact with.**

## One idea, not one player per format

Many systems ship a separate player for each content type, so a new format means new code. In Syncropel, a **new kind of playable media is a record you write, not a player you build.**

Two records describe any medium:

- **`core.medium.v1` describes the content (what).** It points to the content, never holding the bytes. It has a `medium_kind` (such as `audio` or `video`), says where the content is (a mount path, a content hash, a stream), and has a **modality**: `play`, `render`, `run` or `simulate`. The modality tells the screen how to present it.
- **`core.medium.player.v1` describes the player (how).** It links a `medium_kind` to a **playback method** and says how to get the content. Adding a media type means writing one of these.

<Callout type="info" title="Visibility comes from the thread">
A medium never says who can see it. That comes from the thread it lives on, the consent grant over that thread, and, for anonymous playback, a short-lived read token. The same record plays for its owner and, when a public grant exists, for a stranger.
</Callout>

## Playback methods

The playback methods a player can use are a small fixed set:

| Method | For | Support |
|---|---|---|
| **`stream-transport`** | audio and video, streamed in ranges (HTTP `206`) | Supported |
| **`static-render`** | documents, reports, decks | Not supported |
| **`iframe-embed`** | sandboxed embedded pages | Not supported |
| **`wasm-app`** | live apps, games, simulations | Not supported |

Only audio and video (`stream-transport`) play. The other modalities (`render`, `run`, `simulate`) are named but have no playback.

## Playing it for you and for a stranger

For the owner, a medium plays inside your library like any other content. A public link can also make a crate *play* for someone with no account, using:

`GET /v1/public/medium/resolve?thread=&ref=&kind=`

This needs no login, but it is gated by a public consent grant. The requested `ref` must be referenced by a record on the granted thread, so a grant over one crate can't be used to reach other files. It returns the descriptor, a short-lived read token and a read URL. The player streams the bytes in ranges and asks again when the token expires. Revoking the grant ends every outstanding token at once.

This is what makes a shared link *play* rather than just describe. The read token exists because media players can't send an `Authorization` header. It is a credential, not a record.

## In short

- Audio and video are content presented to you and resolved from records when played.
- Two records describe any medium: **`core.medium.v1`** (what) and **`core.medium.player.v1`** (how).
- **`stream-transport`** (audio and video) is the only supported playback method.
- **Anonymous playback** lets a public link play with no account, gated by a consent grant.
