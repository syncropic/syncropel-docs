Agreements fade. Promises drift. Terms of service shift with the wind. But physics? Physics just *is*.

**Physics, Not Policy** is the core design philosophy of the Syncropel Protocol. It means that system guarantees are enforced by **mathematical structure** — not by human processes, legal contracts, or trust in institutions.

## What This Means in Practice

| Policy (Today) | Physics (Syncropel) |
|----------------|---------------------|
| "We promise not to read your data" | Encryption makes reading structurally impossible |
| "We'll pay you when we get around to it" | Atomic settlement means work and payment are one transaction |
| "Our AI won't exceed its budget" | Budget enforcement is a mathematical constraint, not an advisory limit |
| "We track attribution for creators" | Cryptographic lineage makes attribution automatic and tamper-proof |
| "Trust us, it's auditable" | Every state change produces observable, verifiable events |

## The Four Pillars

### Trust
**Verified by proof, not faith.**

Syncropel uses Wilson-score confidence intervals to compute trust mathematically. 3 successes out of 3 attempts rates lower than 95 successes out of 100 — the system accounts for sample size. Trust is not a feeling; it is a statistic.

### Value
**Flows through lineage.**

Every remix is traced. Every creator is paid. Atomic settlement means no reconciliation, no delays, no "oops we forgot." The [Lineage Cascade](/docs/concepts/lineage-cascade) enforces value conservation as a law.

### Rights
**Enforced by code, not law.**

Cryptographic Covenants are rules bound to assets that execute themselves. They cannot be renegotiated without consent because they are structural, not contractual.

### Autonomy
**Bounded by structure.**

[The Dial](/docs/concepts/the-dial) gives you sovereign control over how much autonomy to grant. But the bounds of that autonomy are mathematical — an agent with a budget of $10 cannot spend $11, not because a policy says so, but because the system structurally prevents it.

## Why It Matters

Policy-based systems fail because:

1. **Policies require enforcement** — someone must check compliance
2. **Enforcement requires trust** — you must trust the enforcer
3. **Trust requires verification** — but how do you verify the verifier?

Physics-based systems break this chain. The constraints are the system. There is no enforcer to trust, because there is nothing to enforce — the undesired state is simply unreachable.

## The Gravity Analogy

Like gravity:
- Cannot be bypassed
- Cannot be bribed
- Cannot be forgotten
- Works the same for everyone
- Requires no enforcement agency

The constraints built into Syncropel work the same way.
