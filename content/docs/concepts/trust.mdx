## Overview

Syncropel computes trust from outcomes, not opinions. When an actor completes work and an independent reviewer approves it, that creates a data point. Thousands of data points produce a reliable trust score. This is how the system learns who is good at what.

## Three Dimensions

Trust has three dimensions:

```
trust(actor, domain, judged_by)
```

- **Actor**: who performed the work
- **Domain**: what kind of work (code, ops, data, research, etc.)
- **Judged by**: who evaluated the outcome

A developer might have high trust in `code` but low trust in `ops`. The same work evaluated by different reviewers creates different trust entries. This captures the full picture — not just "is this actor good?" but "is this actor good at THIS, according to WHOM?"

## How Trust Grows

Trust accumulates from the evaluation gate:

1. An actor completes work → claims done
2. An independent evaluator reviews → approves or rejects
3. Approval creates a success observation: `trust += success`
4. Rejection creates a failure observation: `trust += failure`

```bash
spl trust
```

```
ACTOR                   DOMAIN    SUCCESS   TOTAL   TRUST
did:sync:user:alice     code           12      14   0.438
did:sync:agent:dev      code            5       5   0.299
```

The trust score is a statistical lower bound — it reflects confidence based on the amount of evidence. With few observations, trust stays low (conservative). With many observations, trust converges to the true success rate.

## The Dial

Trust feeds into the dial — a continuous value from 0 to 1 that maps to four zones:

| Zone | Range | What It Means | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **REPLAY** | 0.0 - 0.33 | Pattern is proven, replay it directly | Near zero |
| **ADAPT** | 0.33 - 0.50 | Known pattern, minor adaptation needed | Low |
| **EXPLORE** | 0.50 - 0.67 | Moderate novelty, iterative approach | Moderate |
| **CREATE** | 0.67 - 1.0 | Genuinely novel, full reasoning required | Higher |

As an actor builds trust in a domain, their work costs less — proven patterns can be replayed without re-computation. This is the economic engine: every successful completion makes the next similar task cheaper.

## Decay

Trust decays over time. Evidence from 6 months ago carries less weight than evidence from last week. The default decay half-life is 90 days — trust must be continuously maintained through recent work.

This prevents stale trust from persisting. An actor who was excellent a year ago but hasn't worked recently returns to a neutral trust score, requiring fresh evidence.

## Independence Requirement

The evaluator must be different from the executor. An actor cannot approve their own work — self-evaluation is structurally ignored by the trust computation. This ensures trust evidence comes from independent judgment, not self-reported success.
