The **Graph** is how Syncropel workspaces find and reach each other. When two workspaces pair, records flow between them. When many workspaces pair, you get a network of peers over which workspaces, records and queries travel. The Graph is that network. It is not a product, a server, or a place you log in to.

The Graph is not a hub and not the source of truth. Your records are the source of truth. The Graph is one view over them: the connections between workspaces, and the discovery those connections allow.

## A node is just a workspace

Every `spl serve` is already a Graph node. There is nothing extra to install. Pairing your workspace with a peer (see [Federation](/docs/concepts/federation)) joins you to that peer's part of the Graph. Pairing with more peers widens it.

A **public Graph node** is a workspace that has chosen to make some of its records readable without a credential, for example its workspaces (`core.workspace.v1`). `graph.syncropel.com` is one such node: a public index run by the Syncropel team. It is useful, but not special. Anyone can run a node. A company can run a private one for its teams, and a community can run one for its members.

## What the Graph is not

- **Not a hub.** A node publishes records to the network but does not own them. If a node goes offline, the records it published are still in every workspace that fetched them, and can still be verified.
- **Not a gatekeeper.** A node publishes what its owner chooses. It does not approve, deny or rank what is published elsewhere. If a peer publishes a workspace, your workspace fetches it from that peer, not through a central node.
- **Not a marketplace.** There are no payments, ratings, leaderboards or recommendations. A node's selection only means that its owner chose to publish it.

## Discovery is a query

To see the workspaces a node publishes, ask it for records of kind `core.workspace.v1`. This is the same lookup your own workspace runs for [`spl workspace list`](/docs/operate/workspace/lifecycle). A node is an ordinary workspace whose records are readable.

You can also ask a question that reaches across several paired peers at once. Each peer applies its own consent rules, and the query has a budget at every step. This lets a question reach beyond your own records without any peer handing over everything it holds.

## The Graph and your workspace catalog

Don't confuse the Graph with your **workspace catalog**.

- Your **workspace catalog** is the local list of workspaces your own workspace serves. `spl workspace list` shows it. See [Workspaces](/docs/concepts/workspaces).
- The **Graph** is how a workspace published elsewhere can reach you: you pair with that peer, find its workspace by query, and install it into your catalog.

## Why discovery exists

A log of records is complete but doesn't answer "which workspaces should I install?" Someone has to choose what a node recommends, and be accountable for it. On the Graph, that choice is a record on a node, signed by its owner. If you disagree with a node's choices, you follow a different node.

## Seeing across everything you reach

If you run more than one workspace, or others have shared threads with you, **universal traversal** shows all of it as one view. Your own workspaces appear as a single "you", shared threads appear with exactly the access that was granted, and graph queries show how the people and threads in your reach connect. See the [Universal traversal guide](/docs/guides/universal-traversal).
