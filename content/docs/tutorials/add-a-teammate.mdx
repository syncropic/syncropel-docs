## What you'll do

You run a workspace. A teammate, call her Mora, needs to work on it. In this tutorial you will:

1. Issue a **membership invite** and send her the link.
2. Watch her redeem it in the browser and appear on the roster.
3. See her work under her own principal, including signed task verdicts.
4. Renew her membership when the grant expires.
5. Revoke it, and verify her access stops on her very next request.

This is different from [pairing a device](/docs/guides/pair-share-invite): a plain invite creates a credential, while a membership invite creates a **member** with their own identity, a membership grant and a credential tied to that grant. The concept page is [Principals & grants](/docs/concepts/principals-and-grants); this page is the walkthrough.

**Prerequisites**: a running workspace you hold an `admin` bearer for, reachable at a URL your teammate's browser can open.

## 1. Issue the invite

```bash
spl invite create --member --preset contributor
```

```text
✓ Issued membership invite (accepting it creates a member): inv_9f2c41d0b8a7
  link:    https://alice.syncropel.app/i/inv_9f2c41d0b8a7?sig=...
  expires: 2026-08-15T09:12:00Z
  after redemption: `spl member list` shows the new member
```

What the flags mean:

- `--member` makes this a membership invite. Accepting it creates an identity, a membership grant and a credential tied to that grant, not just a bearer token.
- `--preset contributor` sets the membership grant's scopes (`records:read` + `records:write`). `reader` and `admin` are the other presets; `--scopes` overrides a preset with explicit wire strings.
- The invite *link* expires in 24 hours by default (`--ttl` to change). The membership *grant* it will mint lasts 180 days by default (`--grant-ttl`, anywhere from 1 hour to 10 years). These are two different lifetimes: one for the unused link, one for the membership itself.

Send Mora the link over whatever channel you trust. Anyone holding the link before it expires can use it, so treat it like a key, not like a webpage.

## 2. Your teammate accepts it

Mora opens the link. The invite page shows who issued it, what scopes it grants, and asks her to:

- **Choose a member name**: lowercase letters, digits, and hyphens, up to 63 characters (`mora`, `mora-k`). It becomes her DID on your workspace: `did:sync:user:mora`.
- **Accept custodial custody**: the page states plainly that your workspace will hold her signing key and sign on her behalf. This is the only way to join; [Joining a workspace](/docs/get-started/joining-an-instance) covers what it means from her side.

She confirms. Behind the button, the browser calls `POST /v1/invites/inv_9f2c41d0b8a7/redeem` with her `member_label` and `custody: "custodial"`. Your workspace creates her identity, links her name to it, records the membership grant and your acceptance, then creates a service account and bearer token tied to the grant. The page shows her the token once.

## 3. Confirm the roster

Back on your side:

```bash
spl member list
```

```text
did:sync:user:mora                       active     scopes=["records:read","records:write"] expires_at="2027-02-10T09:14:02Z"
```

Her joining also appears in the audit feed:

```bash
spl invite audit
```

```text
2026-08-14T09:14:02Z     redeemed         inv_9f2c41d0b8a7
```

Note the visibility rule: **you see every row because you are an admin.** When Mora runs `spl member list` against your workspace, she sees only her own row. The grant graph at `GET /v1/folds/grant` scopes the same way: admin sees everything, everyone else sees only grants where they are the issuer or the audience.

## 4. Your teammate works

On her machine, Mora saves the bearer so the CLI injects it automatically:

```bash
spl token save spl_prod_sa_XXXXXXXX_YYYYYYYYYYYY
spl task list          # now authenticated as did:sync:user:mora
```

From here she works like anyone else, with one difference that matters: when she reviews work, her verdicts are **signed with her identity key** and checked when they are saved:

```bash
spl task approve TASK-0041 --grade 0.75
```

The verdict record names her as the evaluator, carries her signature, and the workspace checks it against her key. A verdict with a wrong signature is refused. She also cannot approve her own work under another name, because both sides are traced back to the member behind them.

Her bearer has its own expiry (30 days by default) and can be rotated ahead of time. The membership grant lasts longer; the bearer is a renewable credential under it.

## 5. Renew when the grant expires

Six months later the membership grant expires. Mora's next request gets:

```text
401 GRANT_EXPIRED
```

Renewal is for an **expired** grant. While her grant is live she never needs you to get back in: on any thread, **keep this access** makes her a key, and pasting it into Studio on a new device re-credentials her under the same grant (see [Keeping your access](/docs/guides/keeping-your-access)). To renew, issue a fresh invite **pinned to her name**. Only an admin can do this:

```bash
spl invite create --member --label mora
```

She accepts the new link exactly as before. Because the invite is pinned to her name, it becomes a **renewal**: the same member (same DID, same history, same trust) gets a fresh grant and bearer, and the old grant is revoked with reason `membership-renewed`.

Pinning also stops takeovers: an *unpinned* membership invite can never claim an existing name. If someone uses a generic invite and types `mora`, they get `409 MEMBER_LABEL_TAKEN`. Holding an invite link is not enough to become an existing member.

## 6. Offboard

When Mora leaves:

```bash
spl member revoke mora --reason "contract ended"
```

```text
✓ Membership revoked: did:sync:user:mora (grant 7a29c4e1f0b3) — 1 credential(s) killed, 0 sub-grant(s) invalidated; scope: local
```

One command ends everything: revoking the grant revokes **every credential tied to it**, including her bearer and any agent grants she had issued under her membership. Her bearer fails on its very next request:

```text
401 GRANT_REVOKED
```

Three notes on that output:

- **Bilateral.** Mora can revoke her own membership too, because leaving is not something only the owner can do.
- **Idempotent.** Revoking an already-revoked member reports success without erroring.
- **`scope: local`.** The revocation applies to *this* workspace only. Credentials that live on other workspaces are not affected.

The event lands as `member_revoked` on the same audit feed (`spl invite audit`).

## What just happened, in records

Everything above is a handful of records, so you can check each step against the log:

| Step | Record(s) |
|---|---|
| Invite issued | `core.invite.v1` on `th_invites`, carrying a `membership` envelope |
| Redemption | `core.identity.created.v1` (genesis) + `core.identity.binding.v1` (`did:sync:user:mora` → her master DID) + `core.identity.grant.v1` (scopes, `expires_at`, caveats, chain) + `core.identity.acceptance.v1` (custodian-signed), then a service account and bearer tied to the grant |
| Her approvals | Signed verdicts naming her as reviewer, checked when saved |
| Renewal | A fresh grant + acceptance; a `core.identity.revoke.v1` closing the old grant (reason `membership-renewed`) |
| Offboarding | One `core.identity.revoke.v1` naming the grant. Her credentials are revoked along with it |

In short: membership is a grant, and one record ends it.
