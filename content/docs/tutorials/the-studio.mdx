## What you'll learn

The [Quickstart](/docs/start) shows the loop from the command line. This tutorial shows the same instance from the other side: the **Studio**, the web surface your workspace serves. You will touch all three shapes ([the sentence, the ledger, the address](/docs/concepts/the-three-shapes)) in one short pass. You'll read what is there, write by completing a sentence and share a thread knowing exactly what the guest will see.

You need a running workspace. If you followed the Quickstart, you have one:

```bash
spl serve
```

Then open your workspace in a browser. Local workspaces serve the Studio at their own origin, and a hosted workspace serves it at your workspace URL.

## 1. Read a ledger

Open any thread with something on it: a task, a conversation, a shared space. Down the left are your **places** (your call, everything, files). Each is a ledger: a list of rows over what your whole instance holds. Each row is built from records, and a count that might not be the whole set prints with a `+`, so you always know whether you are seeing everything.

Inside a thread, the chips across the top (`invites`, `files` and any your apps add) are the thread's own ledgers. Click one and it takes the stage. Notice the row **verbs**: an invite row can be revoked, a file row can be opened. A ledger does not just show data. It shows what you can do to each row, and only offers a verb it can actually satisfy.

## 2. Write by completing a sentence

You will not find a form. To do anything, you complete a sentence. In the composer at the foot of a thread, type `/`:

```text
/
```

The Studio offers the acts you can perform here. Pick `share` (or `invite`, where an app offers it) and it shows a sentence with blanks:

```text
invite  @who?  as  a guest  for  how long?  to  [[ this thread ]]
```

Fill the blanks. `@` offers people, `for` understands "a week" or "3 days", and `[[` names threads. As you fill them, the line under the sentence tells you in plain words exactly what the act will do:

> makes a guest link: they read everything on this thread, no account, for a week, nobody else changes

Nothing is written until you press the verb. This is the only way to write in the Studio: every change is a sentence you reach the same way.

## 3. Look before you share

Before you hand out that link, see the thread the way the guest will. Choose **see it as a guest** at the top of the thread. The page narrows to exactly what a guest link discloses: only the records that link admits, in the guest's own read-only view. A banner reminds you that you are looking, not editing.

This is your chance to catch a leak before it happens. If the thread carries something you did not mean to share (a note, a number, another person's row), you see it here as they would, instead of finding out after you have sent the link. Press **stop** to return to your own view.

> The preview is faithful, not a mock. It shows the guest's real view over a copy of your own records, limited to what a guest can see, so what you see is genuinely what they get.

## 4. Read the address, and share it

Look at the bar at the bottom. It is not a breadcrumb. It is the **address**, and its value is real:

```text
⌂ the night  ›  invites   as guest
```

It names where you are, what you have open, which view is showing and, while you preview, whose view you are borrowing.

Because the address is a value, it is what you cite and what you share. Copy it and a teammate lands exactly where you are. When you share with someone outside your workspace, the Studio makes a *different* address on purpose: the public link, not the address of the room you are in. That way you can't hand out the wrong door by accident.

## What just happened

You used all three shapes in one pass:

| Shape | What you did |
|---|---|
| **Ledger** | read the rows built from your records, and the verbs on them |
| **Sentence** | filled in the blanks of an act to write one record, the only way to write |
| **Address** | read where you were, previewed as a guest, and got a link to share |

That is the whole surface. There is no fourth kind of screen. Everything else you do in the Studio (running an agent, reading a decision, managing an app) uses the same three shapes over different data.
