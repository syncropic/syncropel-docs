## What it is

**Your call** is the one place that shows what is waiting on you: the things
someone or something in your workspace needs you to settle, ranked so the most
pressing sit at the top.

It exists because the alternative is worse. Work that needs a person tends to
scatter across threads, and anything scattered is eventually missed. One place
means one thing to check.

## Two kinds of item, and they are not the same

Everything in your call is one of two shapes, and the difference matters more
than it looks.

**A decision** needs an answer from you. Someone asked which of two ways to go,
or asked you to supply something only you have. A run may be stopped and waiting
on it right now, which is why decisions are what the count counts.

**A heads-up** needs only a nod. Something happened that you should know about.
Nothing is being asked and nothing is stuck behind it.

They are drawn differently and counted differently on purpose. A number that
folds in everything you could look at will always read heavier than the work
that actually needs you, and a badge that overstates is a badge you learn to
ignore.

<Callout type="info">
If your call says three, three things need an answer. Heads-ups are still
there to read; they are just not part of that number.
</Callout>

## Suggestions sit apart

Your workspace also proposes things: a rule it thinks would help, a pattern it
noticed. These are worth seeing and they are **not** the same as a person asking
you something, so they sit in their own band below rather than in the same
ranked order.

The reason is arithmetic. A workspace can produce hundreds of near-identical
suggestions from ordinary activity, and if they shared a queue with real
questions they would outrank people on volume alone.

Identical suggestions are **folded into one row** with a count, because a
suggestion made two hundred times is one suggestion. Settling any one of them
settles the whole group.

## The order

1. **Anything blocking comes first**, meaning work is stopped until you answer.
2. **Then the longest waiting.**
3. **Then your own order**, if you have set one.

When there is more waiting than can be counted exactly, you will see a figure
with a plus on it. That is a floor, not an estimate: there are at least that
many.

## Answering

Answer a decision where you find it. You do not open anything first, and you do
not go somewhere else to do it, because answering ten things through ten
detours is how a queue stops being read.

Answering can also start work rather than only finish it. The first question a
new workspace asks you is an ordinary decision in this lane, and answering it
opens your first thread, titled with what you typed. If a run was parked waiting
on your answer, it picks up where it stopped.

## From the command line

The same lane, from a terminal. The command is named `register`, which is the
internal name for this list:

```bash
spl register
```

Change how many rows print with `--limit` (30 by default).

### Putting one thing above another

When the automatic order is not the order you want to work in:

```bash
spl register weight <above> <below>
```

Both arguments are item ids, and a prefix is enough. It persists as your own
preference. To take it back:

```bash
spl register weight <above> <below> --withdraw
```

### Turning your assistant off

Your assistant is the member that answers when you say something to your
workspace. It is your preference and you can turn it off:

```bash
spl register listener off
spl register listener on
```

### Saying one thing

`spl say` sends one message to your assistant on a fresh thread and prints the
reply. It is the typed equivalent of the first message in the app:

```bash
spl say "What changed on the roster today?"
```

| Flag | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--thread <THREAD>` | Continue an existing thread instead of opening a new one |
| `--wait <SEC>` | Seconds to wait for the reply (default `90`) |

`spl say` is one round trip. For work you want to sit with, use
[`spl run`](./running-a-goal).

### Getting back in after time away

```bash
spl resume
```

One re-entry summary answering three questions: where you were, what changed,
and what needs you. Point it at one place to get the summary for just that
place:

```bash
spl resume th_auth_refactor_abc123
spl resume SKL-3699
```

## For scripts

Every command here takes `--json` (or `-o json` / `-o stream-json`):

```bash
spl register --json | jq '.data'
spl resume --json
```
