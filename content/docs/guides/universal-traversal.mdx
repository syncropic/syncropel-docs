You can run more than one Syncropel workspace (one on a laptop, one hosted, one for work) and you can pair with other people. **Universal traversal** ties that together: you see *everything you reach* in one view, instead of signing in to each workspace separately.

## Everything you reach

Your **reach** is the union of:

- **Your own workspaces** — every workspace that belongs to *you*.
- **What others have shared with you** — threads other people have granted you access to.

Across your *own* workspaces there are no walls. Because they're all you, you see full detail everywhere: a thread you started on your laptop and one on your hosted workspace sit side by side in one timeline.

Most read endpoints accept a `root` parameter to choose the scope:

- default (no `root`): just this workspace.
- `root=⊤`: your whole reach (all your workspaces plus what's shared with you). The `⊤` symbol (read "top") means "everything."

```bash
# Your home digest across everything you reach
curl "http://localhost:9100/v1/folds/rollup?root=⊤"
```

Each item in the digest says which workspace it came from. If one of your workspaces is unreachable, the response says so instead of quietly leaving out its activity.

## Asking the graph questions

Your reach is a graph of people, the threads they take part in, and the records that connect them. Graph queries let you ask about that structure:

| Question | Query |
|---|---|
| How am I connected to this person? | `op=path&from=…&to=…` |
| What threads do we both touch? | `op=shared_context&a=…&b=…` |
| Who and what is near this thread? | `op=neighborhood&node=…&k=2` |
| Who's most connected in my reach? | `op=centrality` |

```bash
curl "http://localhost:9100/v1/graph/query?op=shared_context&a=did:sync:user:alice&b=did:sync:user:bob&root=⊤"
```

A path can go *through* people you share context with, so "how do I reach Dana?" can answer "through Bob, in the thread you both joined."

## One trust score per person

Someone might reach you from several workspaces (their laptop, their hosted workspace), but they're one person. **Principal trust** combines their track record into a single score instead of one per workspace:

```bash
curl "http://localhost:9100/v1/folds/principal_trust?root=⊤"
```

You get one trust score per *person* in your reach, based on everything they've done anywhere you can see, not one score per device.

## How shared threads appear

When someone shares a thread with you, you see it with **exactly the access they granted**, no more and no less:

- **Granted**: you see the thread at the level they shared it.
- **Sealed**: a thread shared at a summary level shows its outline and a summary, but the full contents stay sealed until access is granted.
- **Omitted**: a thread they *didn't* share doesn't appear at all. It isn't listed as "hidden"; from your side it simply isn't there.

This is the same [consent](/docs/concepts/federation) model that governs all federation, applied across your whole reach. You always see your own content in full; other people's content appears only at the level they chose.
