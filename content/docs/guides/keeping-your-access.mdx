---
title: Keeping your access
description: Add a passkey, keep a recovery key, and see every way back into a workspace, so losing a phone or clearing a browser doesn't lock you out.
---

When somebody lets you into a workspace, your access lives in the browser you
accepted it in. Lose that browser, or open a new phone, and you'd have to ask
for a fresh invite. A **way back in** fixes that: a passkey, a recovery key you
keep yourself, or your Syncropel account on a hosted workspace.

Everything on this page is in the Studio under **You**, in the **Devices and
keys** section for the workspace you're in. To see where you're signed in and
sign a device out, read [Where you're signed in](/docs/guides/where-youre-signed-in).

## Ways back in

**Ways back in** lists what gets you back into this workspace if you lose this
device:

- a **Passkey** row for each passkey, with its name and the day it was added;
- a **Recovery code** row with the day it was made, never the code itself;
- **Your Syncropel account**, on a hosted workspace where your account is linked.

If the list is empty, it says so: "No way back in yet. Add a passkey or make a
recovery code, so losing this device doesn't lock you out."

## Add a passkey

A passkey is kept by your phone or laptop, and your platform carries it between
your devices. Once a workspace has your passkey, important changes there, like
adding a device or inviting someone, ask for it first.

The Studio offers one at the right moment. Right after you sign in on a new
device, join from an invite or confirm it's you at Syncropel, you see **Add a
passkey for this workspace?** Press **Add passkey**, or **Later** to skip it for
now. If you just confirmed it's you with your Syncropel account, the offer reads
**Use a passkey here too**: your account passkey signs you in to Syncropel, and
this one is for this workspace.

<Callout type="info" title="A first passkey needs a recent sign-in">
You can add your first passkey to a workspace only within 5 minutes of signing
in, joining, or confirming it's you. This stops someone who got hold of an open
browser from adding their own passkey and taking the workspace over. If the 5
minutes have passed, the Studio asks you to confirm it's you again first.
</Callout>

### If you're asked to confirm it's you first

A browser that was signed in before passkeys existed sees **Confirm it's you,
then add a passkey**. You only do this once.

- **On a hosted workspace,** press **Confirm it's you**. Syncropel checks it's
  you, the page comes back, and the passkey offer appears.
- **On a workspace you or your organization runs,** pick one of these:
  - **Use a code from another device**: on a device that's already signed in,
    open Devices and keys, choose **Add a phone or another device**, and scan the
    code with this device. See [Add a device](/docs/guides/add-a-device).
  - **Use the command line**: run the command the sheet shows, on the computer
    that runs the workspace. It sits on its own line with a **Copy** button.
  - **Ask the owner for a code**: the workspace owner gives you a short code
    (see [below](#a-code-from-the-owner)). Enter it and press **Continue**.

## Confirming important changes

When you do something that changes who can get in, such as adding or signing out
a device, inviting someone or removing a way back in, the Studio shows a sheet
that names what you're about to do, like "Approve: sign out Chrome on Mac in
Acme". Press **Continue** and your device asks for your passkey.

If you have no passkey yet, the sheet leads with adding one: **Add a passkey for
this workspace to continue**. Press **Add passkey**, or **Not now** to leave the
change undone.

On a workspace you run yourself, if you were never set up as its owner, the sheet
says **Set up your owner account first** and shows one command to run on the
computer that runs the workspace. Run it, then try again.

## Your recovery key

A recovery key is a line of letters and digits you keep somewhere off this
device, like a password manager or on paper. It needs no account and no vendor.
{/* WORDS? story to confirm: story's §4 names this "Make a recovery code" / "Your recovery code" / "I've saved it"; the live Studio still shows "keep this access", "make a key", "your key to this" and "I've kept it". The steps below use the live words. */}

1. In Devices and keys, under **keep this access**, press **make a key**.
2. The Studio shows **your key to this**, once. Copy it and keep it somewhere
   safe, then close it. After that, the Studio can't show it again.

The line looks like this:

```
KRMRW-PEERZ-RHMSW-AH8Y9-1V9S7-K@btw.syncropel.com/<who you are there>
```

From left to right: the key, grouped in fives so it reads aloud; the place; and
who the key opens the place as (shortened here). It isn't a link, so pasting it
into a browser's address bar does nothing.

If you're a guest, the floor is stated beside it: lose the key **and** this
browser, and whoever invited you has to let you in again.

A guest who arrived on an invite link also sees **keep this access** on the
conversation they were invited to.

### Get back in on any device

Open the Studio on the new device, press **+ add a connection**, and paste the
whole line. The Studio offers **open with this key**, and you land in the place
as the same person.

Using the key spends it. Before you land, the Studio shows you a new key, once.
Keep that one, because the old one no longer works.

Typing is forgiving: case doesn't matter, spaces and hyphens are ignored, and a
photographed `O` or `I` is read as `0` or `1`.

### Make a new key

Once you've kept a key, **keep this access** says so and offers **make a new
one**. It asks for the current key first, then shows the replacement once. The
old key stops working. Someone holding a stolen browser can't quietly replace
your key, because they don't have the current one.

## Remove a way back in

Press **Remove** on a row.

- **If it isn't your last one,** the Studio asks **Remove this way back in?** and
  says the others keep working. Press **Remove**, then confirm with your passkey.
- **If it's your last one,** the Studio asks **Remove your last way back in?**
  and warns that losing this device would then lock you out. Type
  `remove my last way back in` in the box. **Remove** stays greyed out until the
  words match. Case and spaces at either end don't matter.

If you just used that way back in to get into the workspace, you can't remove it
from this device. Add another way back in first, or remove it from a device
that's been signed in longer.

## When a passkey is added or removed

When a passkey is added to a workspace for you for the first time, or any of
your passkeys is removed, your **other** signed-in devices show a notice. The
device that made the change doesn't.

- **A passkey was added for this workspace.** It says which device and when. If
  that was you, press **That was me**. If it wasn't, press **It wasn't me**
  before the time shown, which is 24 hours after the passkey was added. You
  confirm it's you, the passkey is removed, and the device that added it is
  signed out.
- **A passkey was removed for this workspace.** If it wasn't you, press **It
  wasn't me** to open Devices and keys. Add a passkey again and sign out any
  device you don't recognize.

A device name chosen by whoever added the passkey is always shown in quotes, as
"a device named “…”", so it can't read like a message from Syncropel.

### Undo from the email

On a hosted workspace, the owner also gets an email when a passkey is added,
with a **Remove this passkey** button. It opens a page that says **Remove a
passkey you didn't add**. Press **Confirm it's you**, sign in to your Syncropel
account if asked, and the passkey is removed and the device that added it is
signed out.

The page tells you if the passkey was already removed, if the 24 hours have
passed, or if you opened the link on the device that added the passkey, which
can't undo it. Open it on another device instead.

After 24 hours, you can still remove a passkey with your own passkey in Devices
and keys.

## A code from the owner

If someone in a workspace you run has no passkey and their browser was signed in
before passkeys, they can't add one alone. You can vouch for them.

1. On their row in the members list, press **Get a code for** and their name.
2. Read the sheet and press **Get code**, then confirm with your passkey.
3. The Studio shows their code, once. Give it to them yourself, in person
   or on a call. It works once, within an hour. Restarting the workspace cancels
   it.

They press **Ask the owner for a code** on their **Confirm it's you, then add a
passkey** sheet, enter it and press **Continue**, and the passkey offer appears.
If the code is mistyped, already used or out of time, they're told to ask you for
a new one.

When the code is used, the person's other devices show **A code from the owner
was used to confirm it's you**, so they'd know if it wasn't them.

## You on the command line

If you use a workspace from both the Studio and the command line, people there
see two entries for you. You can link them so they see one. Linking doesn't
change what either can do. Guests don't see this section.

1. In Devices and keys, under **You on the command line**, press **Link** and
   confirm with your passkey.
2. The Studio shows a command on its own line with **Copy**. Run it where you use
   the command line within 10 minutes. It doesn't share your command-line key
   with the browser.
3. The command line prints two words. Back in the Studio, under **Which two words
   does your command line show?**, choose that pair from the four shown.

The Studio says **Linked.** If you choose the wrong pair, or **None of these**,
nothing is linked and you start again from **Link**. Choosing words, rather than
pressing a plain confirm button, means nobody can link your browser to someone
else's command line.

Once linked, if you're ever erased from the workspace, both entries are erased.
To undo it, press **Unlink**. People see two entries again, though anyone who
saw them as one may remember they're the same person.

## Two more ways back, where the workspace accepts them

### Link to your account

Signed in to a hosted account, **keep this access** also offers **link to your
account**. Your account can then find this key again on any device: under **+
add a connection**, **shared with you** lists the places your account knows, and
**open as you** re-opens one without a line to paste. The account is a way to
find the key, never the key itself.

Hosted workspaces accept it. A self-hosted workspace accepts it only if the
person running it sets up a verifier
([operators](/docs/operate/account-verifier)). Signing out on a shared device
clears what that device held and leaves your account's list untouched.

### Sign in with a passkey on a new device

On another device, paste a kept line into **+ add a connection** and choose
**use a passkey instead**. The line only names the place and who you are there;
its code isn't sent, and your passkey signs you in.

A passkey made on one kind of phone doesn't travel to every laptop, so keep a
recovery key as well. The Studio says so when a passkey is the only key you hold.

## Workspaces on an older version

On a workspace that hasn't been updated to the version with sign-in sessions,
Devices and keys shows the older lists instead: **your keys to this**, where
**remove** deletes a key, and **keys in the wild**, where **revoke** takes one
device out without affecting the others.

## For operators

Nothing to configure for the recovery key; every workspace on a current version
accepts it. Guardians, people you trust to help you back in, are set up from the
command line. The requests behind this page are in the
[API reference](/docs/reference/api#self).
