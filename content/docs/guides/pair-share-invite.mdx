## What this is

<Callout type="info" title="This flow is CLI-first today">
Invites are issued and managed with `spl invite` (or the `/v1/invites/*` API). The invite **redemption** page — what the recipient opens — is a built-in browser page on your instance. Studio's **+ add a connection** pane manages what a person holds (connections, keys, devices), and members with a mint grant make guest links from a thread; issuing invites in bulk is still the CLI. The steps below use the CLI.
</Callout>

`spl serve` gives you a built-in flow for adding either of two things to a running instance:

1. **A new device of yours** — a phone, a tablet, a fresh laptop. It joins as a _guest holder_ — Syncropel mints a **principal** (a custodial anchor, the guest DID kept as its label) under a guest-classed grant, and a bearer parented to that grant for the device; the device stores the credential in browser IDB. Revoking the grant ends the bearer, and the guest can [keep a key](/docs/guides/keeping-your-access) to come back on another device.
2. **A federated colleague** — someone who's already paired with their _own_ instance and wants access to yours. They join as a _federated holder_ — Syncropel verifies a cryptographic attestation signed by their home instance, then mints an instance-bound bearer scoped to whatever you granted.

Both flows share one primitive: `core.invite.v1`. A single body kind, one record on the instance's `th_invites` thread, one signed URL, two redeem paths. This guide is operator-facing.

There is a third invite shape not covered here: the **membership invite** (`spl invite create --member`), whose redemption mints a member principal on the roster — an identity your teammate's reviews are signed with — with scopes for the whole instance rather than one thread. See [Add a teammate](/docs/tutorials/add-a-teammate).

## Who this is for

- Operators who want to add their phone or another device to their instance.
- Operators inviting a colleague to a specific thread or namespace.
- Anyone who needs to revoke access after the fact (lost device, team change, lost laptop).

If you're integrating the invite flow into your own UI or CLI, read the [API reference](../reference/api) for the `/v1/invites/*` routes and the [body-kinds reference](../reference/body-kinds) for the on-the-wire envelope shapes.

## How it works in three minutes

1. You issue an invite from the CLI, choosing:
   - Optional **device label** (`--device-label "iPhone 15"`, `"Alice's laptop"`, `"dev-team-onboard"`)
   - **Access level** — `--preset reader` (records:read), `contributor` (records:read+write, default), or `admin` (full instance ops). The default is contributor, not admin — you have to opt into handing out admin keys.
   - **Expires in** — `--ttl <seconds>` (default 24 hours)
   - **Max uses** — `--max-uses 1|5|10` (one-shot pair is the norm; multi-use is for "hand the QR around the room")
2. Run it → Syncropel emits a `core.invite.v1` record and prints a signed link (the same URL doubles as the QR payload via the API response):

   ```bash
   spl invite create --preset contributor --ttl 900 --device-label "My phone"
   ```

3. The recipient opens the link (or scans the QR):
   - For a **guest** device: the `/i/<invite-id>` preview page generates a fresh keypair in the browser via `SubtleCrypto`, POSTs to `/v1/invites/<id>/redeem`, gets back a bearer, and hands the holder off to `syncropel.com/pair/complete` which writes the credential into IDB and lands them on the workspace.
   - For a **federated** holder: the recipient clicks "Sign in using which identity?", picks a paired profile, their home instance signs an attestation, the issuer verifies the signature against the holder's DID document, and the same handoff completes.
4. Every issued invite shows up in `spl invite list` with its current state — `active`, `redeemed`, `revoked`, `expired`, or `exhausted`. Revoke is one command; bulk-revoke wipes every expired or exhausted invite in one call.

## Step-by-step: pair your phone

1. On the machine that talks to your instance, issue a short-lived one-shot invite:

   ```bash
   spl invite create --preset contributor --ttl 900 --max-uses 1 --device-label "My phone"
   ```

2. The command prints the signed pair link. To get a scannable QR on the same terminal, use `spl pair --device "My phone" --url "<your instance URL>"` instead — it renders the QR directly; the invite link works equally well pasted into the phone's browser.
3. On the phone, open the link (typed, pasted, or scanned). Safari / Chrome opens the preview page → "Pair with this instance" button.
4. Tap it. The browser generates a keypair, calls your instance, gets back a bearer, redirects to `syncropel.com/pair/complete?...`, writes the credential to IDB, lands on your workspace.
5. Back on the desktop, `spl invite list` shows the invite flipped from `active` to `redeemed`.

## Step-by-step: invite a federated colleague

Premise: Bob has his own instance at `bob.syncropel.app` and is already paired to it. Alice wants Bob to see a specific thread on `alice.syncropel.app`.

1. Alice issues the invite from her CLI:

   ```bash
   spl invite create --preset reader --device-label "Bob (federated)"
   ```

   Pick `reader` if Bob only needs to read. For a thread-scoped grant or to restrict the holder type to `federated`, issue via the API instead — `POST /v1/invites` with `scope_target: {"kind": "thread", "thread_id": "..."}` and `accept_holder_types: ["federated"]` (see the [API reference](../reference/api#pair-share-invite)).
2. Alice sends Bob the pair link (Slack, email, signed URL — Syncropel signs the URL so it's tamper-evident).
3. Bob clicks the link. The URL routes him to `syncropel.com/pair?invite=<url>`; the pair page detects `accept_holder_types: federated` and shows the **Federated holder** flow at `/pair/federated`.
4. Bob picks his paired profile (`bob.syncropel.app`). The pair page calls `POST bob.syncropel.app/v1/invites/sign-attestation` — Bob's home instance signs the holder_proof.
5. It then calls `POST alice.syncropel.app/v1/invites/<id>/redeem` with `holder_type: federated`, Bob's DID, and the holder_proof.
6. Alice's instance:
   - Looks up Bob's DID document
   - Verifies the cryptographic signature against the signer key
   - Verifies the issued-at-ms is within the freshness window (±60s)
   - Mints an instance-bound bearer scoped to whatever Alice granted
7. Bob's browser stores the new credential, adds `alice.syncropel.app` to his profile list, lands on Alice's instance.

The audit row on Alice's side shows `holder_did = did:sync:user:bob`, `holder_pubkey = <Bob's key>`, and the granted scopes.

## Revoke + audit

- **Revoke one**: `spl invite revoke <invite_id>`. Idempotent — revoking again returns "already revoked" without erroring.
- **Bulk revoke**: `spl invite bulk-revoke expired` / `spl invite bulk-revoke exhausted` sweeps every invite in that state in one call.
- **See the trail**: `spl invite audit` folds `core.invite.event.v1` records from `th_audit_invites` — every redeem (with holder DID), every revoke (with revoker actor + reason), every refused redeem (with the refusal reason: `expired`, `revoked`, `holder_type_mismatch`, `bad_proof`, `freshness_violation`, etc.).

## Renew your bearer

Long-running devices can rotate their bearer ahead of expiry to slide the window forward: hash the current bearer (sha256) to derive the `token_id`, then `POST /v1/tokens/<token_id>/rotate` with the current bearer in the `Authorization` header — the response carries a fresh bearer with a new expiry. Self-only — Syncropel rejects rotation attempts from a bearer that doesn't match the path.

## Templates

If you find yourself issuing the same shape repeatedly ("dev team onboarding: contributor, 7 days, 5 uses, labeled with date"), save it as a template via `POST /v1/invite-templates` (the desired invite shape as the body) and list your saved shapes with `spl invite templates`.

Templates are stored as `core.invite_template.v1` records on `th_invite_templates` (admin-scoped to your operator); they survive across sessions.

## What Syncropel guarantees

The threat model spells out the guarantees in full. The load-bearing ones:

- **Defense-in-depth**: every operator endpoint checks _both_ the bearer scope _and_ the consent grant. A bearer with `admin` scope but no `th_invites:write` grant cannot issue invites.
- **Federated holder proofs are mandatory, never optional**: redeem requests with `holder_type: federated` MUST carry a valid Ed25519 attestation signed by the holder's home instance, with a fresh issued-at-ms. Syncropel refuses (and audits) on `bad_proof`, `key_unknown`, or `freshness_violation`.
- **Audit thread parity**: every redeem / revoke / refusal on `th_invites` mirrors to `th_audit_invites`. The mirror runs in the same transaction so partial state ("redeem succeeded but audit didn't") cannot happen.
- **Federation pair revoke cascades**: when you delete a federation pair, every federated-holder bearer issued through that pair is revoked in the same transaction. Stops "I removed the pair but their bearer still works" silently broken state.
- **Always-admin auth on operator routes**: even on an instance configured `auth.required = false`, the operator endpoints (`/v1/invites`, `/v1/invites/audit`, `/v1/invites/bulk-revoke`, `/v1/invite-templates`, `/v1/tokens/*/rotate`) refuse anonymous callers. The auth-off master switch covers data-plane endpoints; the admin gate is independent.
